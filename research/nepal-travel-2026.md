# Nepal travel desk — research behind the guide (October 2026)

The Nepal guide (`/travel/nepal/`, content in `site/content/<lang>/travel.json`), the visa-checker
rules for Nepal (`site/content/shared/visa_rules.json`, notes `np_*` in `<lang>/checker.json`) and the
travel-form routing (Nepal → Aryan) were written from the sources below. Sources stay here and are not
shown on the site.

## Visas (checked 8 October 2026)

- **Nepal Embassy in China — visa page** (cn.nepalembassy.gov.np/pages/visa/), the official source:
  - Chinese nationals pay no tourist-visa fee, either on arrival at the authorised entry points
    (Tribhuvan International Airport and the land borders) or from a Nepal mission in China. Other visa
    types pay the standard fee.
  - Tourist visa on arrival, multiple entry: US$30 for 15 days, US$50 for 30 days, US$125 for 90 days.
  - SAARC citizens (except Afghanistan) get up to 30 days free on their first visit in a visa year.
  - No visa on arrival for citizens of Afghanistan, Cameroon, Ethiopia, Ghana, Iraq, Nigeria, Liberia,
    Palestine, Somalia, Swaziland, Syria and Zimbabwe, nor for refugees.
  - Land entry points: Kakarvitta, Birgunj, Belahiya (Sunauli), Nepalgunj, Dhangadhi, Mahendranagar,
    Kodari (Tatopani) and Rasuwagadhi (Kerung).
- **Indian citizens** need no visa. They travel with a passport or an Election Commission voter ID.
- **Online arrival form** (Department of Immigration pre-arrival form). It is filled in before travel;
  kiosks at the airport are the fallback. Travel blogs give the form's validity as 15 days; this is not
  stated on the guide.
- Tourist stays are limited to 150 days a year in total. Extensions are issued in Kathmandu and Pokhara.
- **Business visits**: most visitors attend meetings and fairs on the tourist visa. The business visa is
  for registered foreign investors, on the recommendation of the Department of Industry. *Confirm with
  the team.*

## Getting there

- **From China** (airline schedules and aggregators; confirm when booking):
  - Chengdu: Air China and Sichuan Airlines, about 3h50.
  - Kunming: China Eastern, about 3h25.
  - Guangzhou: China Southern, about 4h45.
  - Lhasa: Sichuan Airlines and Air China.
- **Airports**:
  - Tribhuvan International, Kathmandu: the main gateway.
  - Pokhara International: built with Chinese finance; international service is limited.
  - Gautam Buddha International, Bhairahawa: international service is intermittent.
- **Domestic flights**: Pokhara, Bhairahawa, Bharatpur (Chitwan), Simara (Birgunj) and Biratnagar.
  Lukla flights operate from Ramechhap in season.
- **Air safety**:
  - Every Nepali carrier has been on the EU Air Safety List since 2013. A corrective plan was under EU
    review in 2026.
  - Recent domestic accidents: Yeti Airlines 691 (Pokhara, January 2023) and Saurya Airlines (Kathmandu,
    July 2024).

## Working week and safety

- **Two-day weekend.** On 5 April 2026 the cabinet gave government offices and schools a Saturday and
  Sunday weekend, from 6 April, to save fuel. Office hours are Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. Nepal Rastra
  Bank moved banks and financial institutions to the same week. No order covers private employers, so the
  guide says companies set their own week. Sources: Al Jazeera (5 April 2026), Ratopati, ShareSansar,
  The Himalayan Times. *Check that it is still in force before each season.*
- **Protests.** The September 2025 protests in Kathmandu closed roads and brought down the government.
  The guide mentions them only as the reason for daily checks.
- **Monsoon (June–September).** In 2026, floods and landslides disrupted the China crossings at
  Rasuwagadhi and Tatopani, and Chinese goods were rerouted through Birgunj (Nepali press, September 2026).
- **Trekking.** Since 1 April 2023 a licensed guide is required in national parks and conservation
  areas. The Khumbu (Everest) municipality disputes the rule, so the guide says "most" parks.

## Market and trade

- **Population** is about 29–30 million, and remittances are about a quarter of GDP.
- **Electric cars.** In FY2025/26, electric vehicles were 68.6% of car, jeep and van imports (Himal
  Press, customs data; Meroauto gives about 65% of passenger cars). Chinese-made EVs were 71% of EV
  imports in the first seven months. BYD's Atto 2 and Atto 1 were the top two imported cars. The guide
  rounds this to "seven in ten new cars are electric, and most come from China".
- **Imports through India.**
  - About 77% of imports come from India, and China is Nepal's second-largest trading partner.
  - Birgunj (opposite Raxaul) handles nearly half of all imports. It has the Sirsiya dry port and the
    rail link to the Kolkata and Haldia ports.
  - Other customs points: Bhairahawa, Biratnagar, Kakarvitta and the Kathmandu dry port.
- **Fairs at Bhrikutimandap, Kathmandu (2026 editions).**
  - Kathmandu Business Expo: 28–31 January.
  - Nepal Buildcon: 5–8 February.
  - South Asia Trade Fair: 7–11 May.
  - Consumer Electronics & Home Appliances Expo: 4–6 September.
  - Hometex Nepal: 19–21 December.
  - The 2027 dates on the guide are marked "expected".

## Tourism

- **Arrivals.** 1,158,459 in 2025: India 292,438, the US 112,316. Chinese arrivals are reported
  inconsistently, from 58,684 to 95,480; FY2025/26 gives 109,892.
- **Sights.**
  - Seven World Heritage monument zones in the Kathmandu Valley.
  - Chitwan National Park and Lumbini, both World Heritage sites. Lumbini's monastic zone includes the
    Chinese Zhong Hua temple.
  - Pokhara (Phewa Lake, Sarangkot), Nagarkot, and the Everest mountain flight.
- **Time** is UTC+5:45: 2h15 behind Beijing and 15 minutes ahead of India.
